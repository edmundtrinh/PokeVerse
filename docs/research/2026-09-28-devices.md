# Device research: iPhone 18 Pro, iPhone Duo, and Android foldables (2026-09-28)

Research behind PokeVerse's adaptive layouts. It covers the new devices, Apple's guidance for iPhone Duo, Android 17's large-screen rules, foldables on the web, and what all of it means for an Expo and React Native app.

- **As of 2026-09-28.** iPhone Duo ships on 2026-10-23, and Expo SDK 58 is still in beta, so details may change.
- **"(verify)"** marks a fact that rests on a single secondary source.

**Related:** [device layouts](../architecture/device-layouts.md) · [tech-stack review](../reviews/2026-09-28-tech-stack-review.md) · [decisions (ADRs)](../decisions/) · platform guidance for [iOS](../../.claude/skills/ios-platform/SKILL.md) and [Android](../../.claude/skills/android-platform/SKILL.md)

## Key takeaways

- **Design for size classes, not devices.** iOS 27 makes iPhone apps resizable. iPhone Duo's outer display is compact width and its inner display is regular width. Android 17 ignores orientation and resizability restrictions on large screens.
- **Only native navigation containers get iPhone Duo's vertical side bars.** Custom bars and custom header views stay horizontal, so PokeVerse's JavaScript drawer header won't adapt.
- **Build with Xcode 27.** Apps built with Xcode 26 or earlier don't extend under the status bar and camera on iPhone Duo. For Expo, that means SDK 58 with Xcode 27 build images.
- **The fold is a reserved region.** Reading it, along with the vertical-bar edge, needs a small native module on iOS and Android, and CSS Viewport Segments on the web.
- **Prefer an even number of grid columns,** so content divides cleanly at the fold.
- **The portrait lock won't hold** on a Fold's inner screen or on iPhone Duo, so remove it.

## 3. Designing for iPhone Duo: the HIG

Source: [Human Interface Guidelines: Designing for iPhone Duo](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-iphone-duo), a new page published on 2026-09-09.

> "An app designed for iPhone Duo adapts seamlessly to both displays, providing a continuous experience as the device opens and closes."

### Overview and anatomy
- There are two displays, each with its own front camera. A center hinge supports many ways to hold and position the device.
- Apps built with standard system components that support resizing adapt automatically. The patterns in "Designing for iOS" still apply.
- **Closed:** people use the outer display, and the system puts toolbars and tab bars on the side to maximize vertical space.
- **Open in landscape:** the controls stay on the side.
- **Cameras:** the outer front camera sits in a corner, is always visible, and lines up with the side controls. The inner camera is behind the display, hidden until it's active.

### Poses and size classes
- The device can be partially folded like a book, laid on a surface, or stood on its edge.
- **Don't design a custom layout for each pose.** Use size classes: compact width on the outer display, regular width on the inner display.
- "Don't reinvent your app when it resizes." Let the existing layout expand.
- Preview the poses with **Device Hub** in Xcode.

### Best practices
- **Build to resize.** There are two displays, many poses, and Split View multitasking. Use size classes, layout margins, and safe-area insets, and avoid fixed widths and display-specific dependencies.
- **Keep the experience consistent across displays.** Keep functionality and state the same. Where it makes sense, show an extra level of hierarchy on the inner display: Mail shows a list or a message when closed, and both side by side when open.
- **Keep the same functionality in every pose.**
- **Follow the system's vertical layout** for toolbars, tab bars, and navigation controls.
  - The outer display is wider and shorter than on other iPhones, so controls move to the side.
  - On the inner display in landscape, they stay on the side.
  - Standard components do this automatically.
- **Games** should be playable in every pose. Prefer changing the aspect ratio over letterboxing.

### Vertical controls
- **What moves to the side:** toolbars (including navigation buttons), tab bars, and navigation controls, along with the Dynamic Island and the status bar.
- **The exception is the inner display in portrait,** which keeps horizontal bars.
- **In Split View multitasking,** each app puts its controls along its outer edge.
- **Rules:**
  - Account for the asymmetry by using safe areas.
  - Keep controls consistent across poses.
  - Put primary navigation (Back or Close) at the top, then prominent actions (Done).
  - Prioritize frequently used items with `ToolbarItemVisibilityPriority` / `UIBarButtonItemVisibilityPriority`. Items overflow from the bottom up.
  - Don't override the default bar placement.
  - Consider a full-width layout without bars for immersive UIs, as Calculator does.
  - Group related items with `ToolbarItemGroup` / `UIBarButtonItemGroup`, and keep controls near the content they affect.
  - **Give every item a title and a symbol.** Minimize text-only buttons, which stay horizontal.
- **Compression:**
  - Navigation-focused experiences move toolbar items into overflow, and task-focused ones minimize the tab bar (`ToolbarVerticalCompressionBehavior` / `UIVerticalBarCompressionBehavior`).
  - Use the system overflow menu (`ToolbarOverflowMenu` / `additionalOverflowItems`).

### Dynamic layouts and reserved regions
- Use layout margins and safe-area insets, and no fixed widths.
- **Reserved regions:**
  - the outer front camera: always present, and it expands into the Dynamic Island for Live Activities
  - the inner front camera: only while the camera is active
  - the folding region: when the device is partially open, it divides the inner display into regions
- Alerts, context menus, and sheets move to account for the fold. Split views adapt their column widths to the inner display's symmetry.
- **For custom components:** `ReservedRegion` (SwiftUI) and `UIView.ReservedRegion` (UIKit).
- **Adapting to the fold:**
  - Prefer containers that adapt automatically, like Notes' split view.
  - **In grids, prefer an even number of columns,** so content divides cleanly at the fold.
  - Avoid extreme layout changes when folding; favor small adjustments.

### Split views and arrangement views
- **Split views** expand on the inner display and collapse to one pane on the outer display (`NavigationSplitView` / `UISplitViewController`).
- **Arrangement views** are a container for a primary view and a secondary view:
  - **Split arrangement:** side by side when wide, and stacked when tall.
  - **Overlay arrangement:** stacked; when the device is partially folded, the views move to either side of the fold.
  - The APIs are `ArrangementView` (SwiftUI) and `UIArrangementViewController` (UIKit). HStack and VStack layouts map to split, and ZStack maps to overlay.
  - Keep navigation containers outside arrangement views.

The HIG page links three tech talks: 111466, "Design for iPhone Duo"; 111462, "Raise the bar with iPhone Duo"; and 111463, "Strike a pose with adaptive layouts on iPhone Duo".

## 4. Preparing your app for iPhone Duo: the developer requirements

Source: [Preparing your app for iPhone Duo](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/preparing-your-app-for-iphone-duo).

### Build with the latest Xcode

> "Build your app with the latest version of Xcode to use all of the available screen space on iPhone Duo. When you build with Xcode 26 and earlier, your app doesn't extend under the status bar and camera."

- Content moves between the displays as the device opens, closes, folds, and rotates, so **resizing is the key requirement.**
- Apps that already work on iPad and Mac, or that support resizing in iPhone Mirroring, are "well on your way".

### Layout
- Prefer system layouts and containers: split views, tab bars, arrangement views, and navigation stacks.
- Size views relative to their container, not to fixed iPhone dimensions. Use the scene or view bounds, not the screen.
- Use Auto Layout, and track `horizontalSizeClass` and `verticalSizeClass`.
- **Don't use `userInterfaceIdiom` or `UIInterfaceOrientation` for layout decisions.**

### Which bars go vertical

| Bar | Goes vertical? |
|---|---|
| Bar support built into navigation containers: SwiftUI `toolbar` on a `NavigationStack` or `NavigationSplitView`, or UIKit toolbar items on a view controller inside a navigation controller | Yes, in the poses that call for it |
| Custom bars built on `UIToolbar`, `UINavigationBar`, or `UITabBar` | **No** |
| Inspectors | No; they use horizontal bars |
| Split views | Horizontal for the sidebar and content, vertical for the detail view |
| Sheets on the outer display | Vertical by default. Opt out with `toolbarVerticalBehavior` / `preferredVerticalBarBehavior`. |
| Sheets on the inner display | Horizontal for centered or leading placements, vertical for trailing ones |

- **Detecting it:** `toolbarVerticalEdge` (a SwiftUI environment value) and the `verticalBarEdge` trait (UIKit).
- **Hero images** can extend under a vertical bar with `backgroundExtensionEffect()` / `UIBackgroundExtensionView`.
- **Organizing items:**
  - Use semantic placements: `topBarPinnedTrailing` or `pinnedTrailingGroup` for Done, and `cancellationAction` or `leadingItemGroups` for a custom Back or Close.
  - `axisBehavior` controls whether an item joins vertical layouts.
  - `visibilityPriority` sets the overflow order.
  - `ToolbarOverflowMenu` / `additionalOverflowItems` put items straight into the overflow menu.
- **Icons and titles:**

| Where the item appears | What it shows |
|---|---|
| A vertical bar | The icon |
| A horizontal bar | The icon or the title, preferring the icon |
| The overflow menu | Both |
| Items with only a title, or a custom view | **Never shown vertically** |

### Arrangement views
- They come in split and overlay styles. Limit the axis with, for example, `.arrangementViewStyle(.split.axes(.horizontal))`.
- Don't put one inside a navigation split view, a list, or a scroll view.

### Reserved regions
- **Divisions** are the fold, and **occlusions** are the cameras. The outer camera always occludes; the inner camera occludes only while it's active.
- **SwiftUI:** `GeometryReader` → `GeometryProxy.reservedRegions(kind:options:layoutDirectionBehavior:)`. **UIKit:** `reservedRegions(kind:options:)`.
- Regions can be active or inactive. The fold region is active when the device is partially open, and inactive when it's fully open.

### Cameras
- There are outer, inner, and rear cameras, and the display in use can change mid-session (see "Choosing a camera by the direction it faces").
- When the device is fully open and using the rear camera, an app can also show content on the outer display (see "Registering a camera capture accessory on iPhone Duo").

## 5. What it means for Expo and React Native

### Use native containers and Native Tabs
- Only bars from native containers go vertical, so PokeVerse's navigation should be built from them: Expo Router's native stack and [Native Tabs](https://docs.expo.dev/router/advanced/native-tabs/), which arrived in SDK 55.
- In community reports from Expo apps, the tab bar moved to the side on iPhone Duo automatically, while custom header views stayed horizontal (verify with Native Tabs on SDK 58).
- **Today's app won't adapt.** It uses a JavaScript drawer with a JavaScript header, and the Pokédex settings button is a custom `headerRight` view (`App.tsx:22-36` and `:146-181`, on `main` as of 2026-09-28). Custom header views stay horizontal (review finding F26).

### Make header buttons real bar items
- iOS 27 only moves real `UIBarButtonItem`s into the vertical region. In community reports, custom React header views stayed horizontal while the tab rail went vertical.
- **The fix:** declare header buttons with expo-router's `unstable_headerLeftItems` and `unstable_headerRightItems` on iOS, so they become native bar items. These are `unstable_` APIs, so expect them to change.
- Give every item a title and an SF Symbol, and avoid text-only buttons.
- **Examples:**
  - [meetcal/meetcal-app#58](https://github.com/meetcal/meetcal-app/pull/58), "Build for iPhone Duo on Expo SDK 58 preview"
  - [nemu-pm/nemu#34](https://github.com/nemu-pm/nemu/pull/34)

### Reserved regions need a native module
- React Native has no built-in API for `UIView.ReservedRegion` or the `verticalBarEdge` trait. Safe-area insets cover part of the need, but not the fold's division or the camera occlusions.
- **The proposed approach:** a small local Expo Module, `modules/fold-aware`, behind our own `usePosture()` interface. It reads:
  - reserved regions and the vertical-bar edge on iOS
  - `FoldingFeature` on Android (§6)
  - Viewport Segments on the web (§7)
- The details are in the [device layouts](../architecture/device-layouts.md) doc and [ADR-0011](../decisions/ADR-0011-adaptive-layouts-and-foldables.md).

### Build with SDK 58 and Xcode 27
- **SDK 57** is the current stable release: React Native 0.86.3 and React 19.2.3 ([changelog](https://expo.dev/changelog/sdk-57)). Builds made with Xcode 26 won't extend under the status bar and camera on iPhone Duo.
- **The SDK 58 beta** came out on 2026-09-15 ([changelog](https://expo.dev/changelog/sdk-58-beta)):
  - "SDK 58 is built for iOS 27, which requires the UIKit scene-based life cycle and makes iPhone apps resizable."
  - The Expo CLI detects Device Hub, which replaces Simulator.app in Xcode 27, and there's a new `expo-device-hub` plugin.
  - **Breaking changes:** the scene lifecycle; React Native's Strict TypeScript API, now on by default; and `File.write()` in `expo-file-system`, which is now async. `AppMetrics` is deprecated in favor of Observe.
  - The beta lasts 3–4 weeks, and stable ships shortly after React Native 0.88 does.
  - Pre-release versions on Expo's main branch (as of 2026-09-28, and likely to change): React 19.3.0, Reanimated 4.7.0, worklets 0.13.0, gesture-handler ~3.2.1, screens ~4.28.0, and expo-router ~58.0.9.
  - **EAS Build images with Xcode 27 are "coming soon".** The `latest` image currently ships Xcode 26.6.
- **SDK 55** is where Expo Router added Native Tabs, zoom transitions, `Stack.Toolbar` (iOS), and an experimental split view ([changelog](https://expo.dev/changelog/sdk-55)).

### An early community library
- [react-native-duo](https://github.com/CAWRESTLER/react-native-duo) is MIT-licensed, but very early: 0 stars and 5 commits as of 2026-09-28.
  - **Hooks:** `useDuo`, `useDuoHinge`, `useDuoReservedRegions`, and `useDuoCameras`.
  - **Components:** `DuoArrangementView`, `DuoAdaptiveToolbar`, and `DuoCameraView`.
  - **Requirements:** the New Architecture, Xcode 27.1+, iOS 27.1+, and Expo SDK 57+ with scene configuration. It doesn't run in Expo Go.
  - It has no Android implementation; there are only JavaScript fallbacks.
- **Recommendation:** watch it and learn from it, but keep our own `usePosture()` interface, so the implementation underneath can be swapped.

### Checklist for PokeVerse on iOS
- Remove `orientation: portrait` from `app.json`.
- Size from the window (`useWindowDimensions`) or the container, never from the device model or the orientation.
- Build navigation from native containers, with header buttons as native bar items.
- Use an even number of grid columns whenever there's a fold.
- Build with SDK 58 and Xcode 27 before testing on iPhone Duo.

## 6. Android foldables

### Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8, Fold8 Ultra, and Flip8
- Unveiled on 2026-07-22 at Unpacked in London, and released on 2026-08-07. They were the first phones with **Android 17** preinstalled ([Samsung Newsroom](https://news.samsung.com/us/samsung-galaxy-z-fold8-ultra-fold8-flip8-foldables-perfected-every-way-of-living/)).
- The Fold8 is described as a "wide-screen foldable", with a 5.5" cover screen and a 7.6" inner screen (verify). The Fold8 Ultra is the productivity model.
- The Flip8 is a clamshell. Half-folded use, which Samsung calls Flex Mode, is the half-opened posture below.
- The same rules apply to other Android foldables, such as Google's Pixel Fold line.

### Android 17 (API 37)
- **"Restrictions on orientation and resizability are ignored"** on large screens (sw600dp and up), with no documented opt-out ([Android 17](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/17)).
- Android 16 (API 36) introduced this, with a temporary opt-out. The rule applies according to the app's target API level (verify which level Expo's default builds target).
- The docs push an "adaptive-first development standard".
- **For PokeVerse:** once the app targets these API levels, its `orientation: portrait` setting stops applying on a Fold's inner screen.

### Reading the fold: Jetpack WindowManager
- The chain is `WindowInfoTracker` → `WindowLayoutInfo` → `FoldingFeature`, which reports:
  - `bounds`: where the fold or hinge is
  - `state`: `FLAT` or `HALF_OPENED`
  - `orientation`: whether the fold runs vertically or horizontally
  - `isSeparating`: whether it splits the window into two areas
  - `occlusionType`: whether the hinge hides content
- **Postures:** half-opened with a vertical fold is "book" posture; half-opened with a horizontal fold is "tabletop".

### Window size classes

| Class | Window width |
|---|---|
| Compact | Under 600dp |
| Medium | 600–839dp |
| Expanded | 840–1199dp |
| Large | 1200–1599dp (verify) |
| Extra-large | 1600dp and up (verify) |

- React Native's `useWindowDimensions()` reports density-independent pixels on Android, so it maps directly onto these classes.
- PokeVerse's planned `useWindowClass()` uses 600, 840, and 1200 as its breakpoints, and never branches on the device model.

### For PokeVerse on Android
- **Navigation:** a bottom bar on compact windows, and a navigation rail from medium up.
- **The fold:** `modules/fold-aware` reads `FoldingFeature`, behind the same `usePosture()` interface as on iOS.
- **Continuity:** keep state intact when the device folds or unfolds.

## 7. Foldables on the web

- **CSS Viewport Segments** describe the screen regions on either side of a fold: the `env(viewport-segment-*)` environment variables, and media queries such as `@media (horizontal-viewport-segments: 2)`.
- **The Device Posture API** reports whether the device is folded (verify the current API shape and browser support).
- **Treat both as progressive enhancement:** feature-detect them, and fall back to the normal responsive layout.
- **Mobile web basics still apply:** respect `env(safe-area-inset-*)` and `100dvh`, and avoid hover-only interactions.
- **Testing:** Chrome DevTools can emulate foldable devices.

## 9. Implications for PokeVerse

- **`useWindowClass()`:** derived from the window width. Compact is under 600, medium is 600–839, expanded is 840 and up, and large is 1200 and up. Never branch on the device model.
- **`usePosture()`:** returns the state (`flat`, `book`, `tabletop`, or `closed`), plus the hinge, occlusions, and the vertical-bar edge, from `modules/fold-aware`.
- **Layout components:**
  - `<AdaptiveSplit>`: one pane on compact windows, two from medium up, split at the hinge.
  - `<AdaptiveGrid>`: an even number of columns whenever there's a fold.
  - `<SafeContent>`: applies safe-area insets, including side-mounted bars.
- **Delight moments:**
  - a binder that opens like a real one, with a two-page spread and the fold as its spine
  - a DS-style tabletop battle calculator, with results on the top half and controls on the bottom
  - state that survives folding and unfolding
- The full plan, screen by screen, is in [device layouts](../architecture/device-layouts.md) and [ADR-0011](../decisions/ADR-0011-adaptive-layouts-and-foldables.md) (adaptive layouts).
